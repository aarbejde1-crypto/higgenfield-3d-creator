# Why a stock can gap down 40% before the market even opens

**Channel:** finance / investing education, faceless · **Duration:** 40s (4 blocks) ·
**Aspect:** 9:16 · **Pace target:** 2.9 wps → 27–31 words per block

**Through-line — THE PRICE COLUMN.** A solid stacked column whose height *is* the
price. It stands tall and still (calm) → a barrier holds a growing mass back
(escalating) → it snaps and its middle vanishes into a void (peak) → it re-forms
short and settles (resolved). Deliberately textless: the void between the two
halves *is* the gap, so the visual carries the idea without a single word on screen.

---

## Block 1 — 0:00–0:10 — HOOK

> Your stock closed at fifty dollars. It opens at thirty, and nobody traded a single
> share in between — that's a gap down, and the chart simply skips the space. **(29)**

- **SHOT 1 — 0.0s to 2.0s — WIDE, low angle:** the price column stands tall on an empty near-black plane; a hard marker line sweeps in from the left and **snaps** onto its top edge. *(impact beat — this is the cover frame)*
- **SHOT 2 — 2.0s to 4.0s — ECU, high angle:** the marker line resting on the column's top edge, slow push-in.
- **SHOT 3 — 4.0s to 6.0s — MEDIUM, eye level:** a second marker line drops in far below the first and holds in empty space, gentle drift.
- **SHOT 4 — 6.0s to 8.0s — WIDE, low angle:** both lines in frame, the gulf between them dark and empty; whip pan across the void.
- **SHOT 5 — 8.0s to 10.0s — CU, low angle:** the column's base with the lower line slicing through it, static.

## Block 2 — 0:10–0:20 — BUILD

> Markets close at four. The company's bad news lands at six, and every holder who
> wants out can only queue an order — no buyer meets them until morning. **(28)**

- **SHOT 1 — 10.0s to 12.0s — CU, eye level:** a shutter **slams** down in front of the column. *(impact beat)*
- **SHOT 2 — 12.0s to 14.0s — WIDE, high angle:** behind the shutter, small angular shapes accumulate — first a few, then a cluster, then a wall, slow push-in.
- **SHOT 3 — 14.0s to 16.0s — ECU, low angle:** individual shapes pressing into the shutter, deforming it, static.
- **SHOT 4 — 16.0s to 18.0s — MEDIUM, eye level:** the shutter bowing inward while the column behind stays untouched, gentle drift.
- **SHOT 5 — 18.0s to 20.0s — WIDE, low angle:** the full mass stacked against an unmoving shutter; one pulse of light runs its length.

## Block 3 — 0:20–0:30 — TURN

> Here's the twist: the open isn't a trade. It's an auction that matches every queued
> order at one single price, and thirty is simply where buyers and sellers finally
> meet. **(30)**

- **SHOT 1 — 20.0s to 22.0s — MEDIUM, low angle:** the shutter **vanishes in one frame** and the mass surges forward. *(impact beat)*
- **SHOT 2 — 22.0s to 24.0s — ECU, high angle:** shapes funnel toward one narrow seam from both sides — a few first, then a stream.
- **SHOT 3 — 24.0s to 26.0s — WIDE, eye level:** the seam ignites into a single bright horizontal line across frame, slow push-in.
- **SHOT 4 — 26.0s to 28.0s — CU, low angle:** the column **snaps** at that line, its upper section detaching and gone. *(impact beat)*
- **SHOT 5 — 28.0s to 30.0s — MEDIUM, high angle:** the void where the column's middle was, nothing crossing it, gentle drift. *(CTA lands here — ~75%)*

## Block 4 — 0:30–0:40 — PAYOFF

> So the drop didn't start at the open — it built overnight, in orders nobody could
> fill. Remember: the chart skips the space, because the selling was already done. **(28)**

- **SHOT 1 — 30.0s to 32.0s — CU, eye level:** a shortened column re-forming bottom-up, blocks stacking in fast, static.
- **SHOT 2 — 32.0s to 34.0s — WIDE, low angle:** the short column beneath the empty void; whip up into the void.
- **SHOT 3 — 34.0s to 36.0s — ECU, high angle:** the void's clean upper edge, nothing there, slow push-in.
- **SHOT 4 — 36.0s to 38.0s — MEDIUM, eye level:** both marker lines, the gap between them **stamped** shut with one hard mark. *(impact beat)*
- **SHOT 5 — 38.0s to 40.0s — WIDE, eye level:** tall void, short column, both markers — easing into a settled, stable hold.
